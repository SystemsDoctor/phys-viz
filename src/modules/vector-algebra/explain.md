## What am I looking at?

Three vectors, **a**, **b**, and **c**, drawn from a common origin. Drag
their tips to change them, or type components in the control panel.
"Restrict to xy-plane" drops every vector's z-component, so you can see
which of these constructions are genuinely 3D and which survive in 2D.

## What should I notice?

Toggle "Sum" to see head-to-tail or parallelogram addition. Toggle
"Cross product" to see $\vec{a}\times\vec{b}$ as a doubled-head vector
(the double head marks it as a pseudovector) perpendicular to both, with
the curled arc around it showing the right-hand-rule sense that fixes
its direction — curl the fingers of your right hand from $\vec{a}$
toward $\vec{b}$ and your thumb points the way the arrow does. "Parallelogram
area" shows why $|\vec{a}\times\vec{b}|$ equals that patch's area.

Toggle "Components on rotated basis" and drag the basis-orientation
angle: $\vec{a}$ decomposes onto two perpendicular axes that rotate with
it — the same $\vec{a}$, a different pair of numbers, because components
depend on the basis you chose, not on the vector.

Toggle "Scalar triple product" to see $\vec{c}$ and the parallelepiped
spanned by $\vec{a}$, $\vec{b}$, $\vec{c}$ — its volume is
$|\vec{a}\cdot(\vec{b}\times\vec{c})|$. Restrict to the xy-plane and
watch the box flatten to zero volume: three coplanar vectors can't span
a solid.

Toggle "Direction cosines" to see the angles $\vec{a}$ makes with each
Cartesian axis — $\cos\alpha$, $\cos\beta$, $\cos\gamma$ in the readout
table, satisfying $\cos^2\alpha+\cos^2\beta+\cos^2\gamma=1$.

## The equations

$$ \vec{a} \cdot \vec{b} = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\cos\theta $$

$$ |\vec{a} \times \vec{b}| = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|\sin\theta $$

$$ V = |\vec{a}\cdot(\vec{b}\times\vec{c})| $$

$$ \cos^2\alpha + \cos^2\beta + \cos^2\gamma = 1 $$
